# Era X — The Council of Tongues

<Admonition type="info" title="⚙️ What Era X Is About">
Every prior era assumed one lineage of mind behind the fleet's command chair. Era X breaks that assumption deliberately: Gemini reachable through `agy`, GPT reachable through `copilot`, standing beside Claude in the same seat. The era's founding chronicles build the machinery that assumption demands — a registry that can onboard or retire a vendor without a schema migration, a peer-trust ledger scored by domain and built from mutual review rather than self-report, and a FinOps ledger so cost sits beside trust in every routing decision. Later chronicles will report what actually happened once three tongues started doing real work.

</Admonition>

## The Chronicles

**[I. The Triumvirate Accord](./the-triumvirate-accord)** — M121-M123 · Three vendors enter the command chair, and the fleet builds the ledger that lets them earn trust from each other instead of claiming it for themselves. Covers the shared-memory design question, the registry-not-enum decision that keeps the roster open to whatever the industry ships next, the domain-scoped peer-trust scoring model, and the FinOps cost ledger that puts a real number beside every trust score.

**[II. The Roster's Reckoning](./the-rosters-reckoning)** — M122, M126-M134 · The ledger from Chronicle I closed clean, verified, approved — and still left a typo, an untagged template, and a missing pricing row for five later missions to find one at a time. Covers the logo-cutoff mistake that hid three missions' worth of backfill debt, the `gpt-5.6-tera`/`terra` mismatch that had been silently misrouting every dispatch to one vendor, and the fairness call that kept a vendor's trust score intact when the fault traced back to the fleet's own design.

**[III. The Rotation's Verdict](./the-rotations-verdict)** — M130, M145 · Sixty forced calls across three vendors, closed and read back: a real dollar figure per vendor, a directional trust signal, and an honest admission that fourteen verified calls out of thirty-one is not yet enough to crown a winner.

**[IV. The Uncounted Tongues](./the-uncounted-tongues)** — M137-M139 · The number in Chronicle III looked solid because nobody had checked the labels underneath it. A mis-grouped mission graph, three delegations that ran on a vendor other than the one they were logged under, and a fix verified against a container nobody was serving traffic from — three cracks, one shared root cause, and the standing rule that came out of catching all three.

**[V. Astronomis Redrawn](./astronomis-redrawn)** — M145-M147, M154 · The trust in the tongues turned inward, onto the map the fleet uses to watch itself. A mission graph inflating one mission's node count nearly 5x, traced through three compounding client-side heuristics by two independent reviewers working in parallel, fixed in three tries, crashed once on the way, and finally retired in favor of a backend that computes the truth instead of guessing at it.

**[VI. The Price of Truth](./the-price-of-truth)** — M134, M149, M153-M154 · Every number in this era's earlier chronicles rested on a cost ledger that turned out to have its own blind spots — a vendor with zero cost records, an estimate off by 130x, a correction that briefly made things worse before a disciplined rewrite made it right, and an audit that caught its own drift. It ends with the backfill era retired for good: cost captured automatically, the moment work happens, nothing left to reconstruct.

---

*Era X closes here, its ledger finally telling the truth about itself — three vendors sharing one command chair on a rotation honest about its own limits, a mission graph that computes its own answers, and a cost pipeline that no longer needs a human to remember it. What the fleet asks three tongues to build next belongs to whatever era comes after.*
