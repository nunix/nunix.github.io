Ten eras taught the fleet to build. This one taught it to look back. Universalis remembers everything it is told, but memory and awareness are not the same thing — a table can hold a record for months while nothing that reads it ever mentions its name. Era XI is the era of finding those tables again: an audit that predated its own audit table by two and a half months, a quality patrol quietly closed and never counted, a rotation ledger that ran out and stayed forgotten for a week. None of these worlds were destroyed. They were simply no longer visited. This is the chronicle of walking back into them.

# Era XI — Imperium Lost Worlds

<Admonition type="info" title="⚙️ What Era XI Is About">
Every prior era added something new to the fleet — a ship, a vendor, a ledger, a language. Era XI adds nothing. Instead it audits what already exists, and finds that "existing" and "known" had quietly drifted apart. A table doesn't announce when it stops being read. A number doesn't announce when it stops being counted. The founding chronicle of this era is the story of the Inquisitor itself — the fleet's own auditor — discovering that its own history was incomplete, and what it took to make the count honest again.

## The Chronicles

**[I. The Forgotten Verdicts](./the-forgotten-verdicts)** — M37, M50, M162–M164 · The Inquisitor's own audit table remembers three audits — until someone asks why the third one calls itself "third" when the fleet's oldest quality report is nine weeks older than the table itself. Covers the informal M37 audit an ally ship ran when the captain skipped its own review process, the QP-01 quality patrol that formalized it, the automated table built two and a half months later with no memory of either, and the single-column bug that could erase a working feature the moment its own history was restored.
