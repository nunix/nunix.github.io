<Admonition type="note" title="📜 REMEMBRANCER'S NOTE — Era XII">

Eleven eras of this chronicle were written about software the fleet built for itself. This one begins with something the fleet did not build at all.

Omarchy is another forge's work — an opinionated Arch Linux desktop, Hyprland shaped into something you can hand to a person who has never tiled a window in their life. The fleet wanted it. The fleet does not run Arch. What follows is the record of taking a machine spirit built for one forge world and teaching it to breathe another's air: a package manager that answers to a name it was never given, a configuration format that changed underneath the port halfway through, and a workstation whose graphics card spent a year lying about how fast it could draw.

Nothing in this era is finished. That is deliberate. The migration is still running as this is written.

*— The Remembrancer of the AIverse*

# Era XII — The Grafted Forge

<Admonition type="info" title="⚙️ What Era XII Is About">
Every prior era asked what the fleet should build. This one asks a narrower and more awkward question: what do you do when the thing you want already exists, and it was built for someone else's system? Porting is not copying. It is a long argument with every assumption the original author never had to write down.

*[Return to the Cosmic Map](/cosmos) to see all eras.*
</Admonition>

## The Chronicles

**[I. What We Took From Another Forge](./what-we-took-from-another-forge)** — M60, M171 · Two projects with opposite philosophies — Arch's hand-assembled minimalism and Tumbleweed's tested rolling snapshots — and the argument for taking the desktop from one and the substrate from the other. Why the fleet wanted Omarchy's opinions but not Omarchy's distribution, what "opinionated" actually buys a user, and the moment a port stops being a copy and starts being a fork with obligations.

**[II. The Shim That Spoke Pacman](./the-shim-that-spoke-pacman)** — M171 · A desktop that calls `pacman` several hundred times cannot be ported by rewriting every call site. The alternative is to make `pacman` exist. Covers the translation shim, the AUR that has no Tumbleweed equivalent and the OBS that almost does, why the shims moved out of `/usr/bin`, and the difference between an installer that works on the author's machine and one that survives `curl | bash` on a stranger's.

**[III. The Migration That Is Still Running](./the-migration-that-is-still-running)** — M60, M171 · Upstream changed its configuration language from `.conf` to Lua in the middle of the port, and a half-migrated desktop looks exactly like a working one until it doesn't. Covers the v3 files that shadowed their v4 replacements, the config error that painted a red bar across a running desktop for hours, and why a migration you can't tell is unfinished is the most expensive kind.

**[IV. Seven and a Half Seconds a Frame](./seven-and-a-half-seconds-a-frame)** — M170 · A remote desktop that four rounds of tuning could not fix, because none of the tuning touched the thing that was broken. Covers the measurement that ended the argument, why a 10-gigabit switch was irrelevant, the split-package trap that made a driver look impossible to install, and a 236× speedup that no configuration file could have delivered.
